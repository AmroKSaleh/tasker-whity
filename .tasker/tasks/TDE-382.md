From Linear: Guided Reviews (2026-05-27, with Linear Diffs) restructure a PR into a narrative — core of the change first, per-section explanations (what changed → consequences), glue/secondary code separated out — so review shifts from error-hunting to judgment. Their companion essay's thesis: agents made review the bottleneck (50% more PRs Jan–Mar 2026); humans should judge fit, not hunt typos. That thesis IS our positioning ("quality-gated").

Build: the existing task judge (review_task / get_task_critique) emits a structured review narrative instead of a flat verdict blob: the core claim/change first, then consequence-ordered sections, then secondary noise separated at the bottom. Verdict logic unchanged — presentation upgraded so the human gate takes 30 seconds of judgment. Render in the inspector on the needs-review card.

We do NOT build a diff/code-review surface (duplicates GitHub; team-scale). Cross-ref TDE-261 (judge design, open) and TDE-348 (needs-review card state, in progress).

PROMOTED FROM PRESENTATION TO STRUCTURAL (2026-07-28, from the Flows redefinition agenda — Q7/TDE-799).

This was filed as "restructure judge output for the human gate" — a readability improvement. Q7 makes it the mechanism the whole gate design depends on.

The reasoning: human attention does not merely run out, it DEGRADES with volume (anyone reviewing 29 step outputs is rubber-stamping by the sixth). A rubber-stamped output is worse than an ungated one, because it propagates with confidence behind it. So per-step human approval cannot work at any flow length worth calling a flow.

The only shape where broad human gating and a human who is still paying attention coexist: **show the human EXCEPTIONS, never outputs.** Failed checks with their observed evidence, plus the residue of criteria that could not be reduced to a check — "step 3 failed rule 2, here is what was observed", not "here is step 3's output, approve it". The review load then scales with problems found rather than with flow length.

Without this, Q7's model collapses back into per-step approval. Priority raised from medium to high. Read TDE-799 and TDE-797 before building it.

### NEW TOOL: get_flow_exceptions
Three categories, straight from Q7's three acts of human attention:
1. **FAILED CHECKS** — with `observed_value` surfaced, because a claim that something failed is not reviewable; what was actually seen is. Shows the consuming step and the retry count. When no observed_value was recorded it says so explicitly ("the check was asserted, not run") rather than hiding it.
2. **JUDGMENT RESIDUE** — every judgment-kind output rule with no independent pass. Q7: "What cannot be reduced to a check IS the human's review list." These appear even when nothing has failed, because nothing has VERIFIED them either. Suppressed once a non-self, non-unverified validator has ruled.
3. **TERMINAL OUTPUT** — steps nothing in-flow consumes, listed unconditionally regardless of check results, because no downstream receiver exists to catch them.

**Blast-radius ordering** implements Q7's placement rule ("early seams in long flows, and irreversible actions") with the one half that is computable: transitive descendant count. A bad output early in a long chain contaminates everything downstream; the last step contaminates only itself. Irreversibility is NOT detectable from the graph and is deliberately not guessed at.

**`include_history:true`** pulls earlier failed attempts from `task_events`. Worth noting: this was impossible before TDE-818 landed this morning — attempt N overwrote attempt N-1, so "what did it fail on the first time" had no answer. That dependency resolved silently today.

Also added the global directive "NEVER ASK A HUMAN TO REVIEW OUTPUTS — SHOW THEM EXCEPTIONS", including the authoring corollary: every criterion converted to kind=check is one the human never looks at again on every seam on every run, so interrogate each with "how would we actually check this?" and record kind=judgment only when the answer is genuinely nothing.

### VERIFIED LIVE on a purpose-built mixed flow (since deleted)
Three chained steps carrying, deliberately: a failing check, a passing check, an unreduced judgment rule, and a terminal step.
- Failed check surfaced with `observed: 11 words (wc -w)`, its note, the consuming step, and "2 retries so far".
- Judgment residue surfaced with its rule text and why it needs a person.
- Terminal correctly identified as step 3 (nothing consumes it).
- **The PASSING check on step 2 does not appear anywhere** — that suppression is the whole mechanism working.
- `include_history:true` recovered BOTH earlier attempts including attempt 1.
- Blast ordering put step 1 (2 descendants) above the terminal step (0).
Type errors unchanged at the pre-existing baseline of 32.

### Honest note on the volume claim
On this 3-step fixture the everything-view was 30 lines vs 20 for exceptions — not an impressive ratio, and I am not going to pretend it is. The argument is structural, not measured: `get_flow_audit` grows per STEP, `get_flow_exceptions` grows per PROBLEM. At Q7's stated scale (29 steps) they diverge sharply; at three steps they cannot. The real evidence here is the suppressed passing check, not the line count.
