Step 11, the capstone. Two scopes: (a) a SINGLE run — what does 'this flow succeeded' mean (all gates passed? artifact met contract? human accepted?); (b) the FEATURE — how do we know flows are worth their complexity at all (ties back to TDE-212 + Round 2: if the distinctive machinery isn't proven to help, the honest verdict may be 'smaller than we thought'). OUTPUT = success criteria at both scopes + a synthesis note pulling Q1–Q11 into the final definition.

---

## OUTPUT — success (settled 2026-07-28)

### Half this question is DROPPED as redundant
"How do you know ONE run worked" needs no definition — Q7 already answers it: the human gates the terminal output and accepts it or doesn't. The user's objection was correct: *success is determined by the user when they get the output.* Nothing further to define.

### The half that matters: is the FEATURE worth having?
Not a question about user satisfaction. Three live decisions depend on the answer:
- the agenda's own open consequence — flows may need **rebuilding** around a new centre. Weeks of work. Without a measure you rebuild, it feels better, and you learn nothing.
- **TDE-791** is an experiment, and an experiment without a pass condition set BEFOREHAND gets rationalised afterwards. Q4 and Q5 are both waiting on its verdict.
- **Q9 made a falsifiable claim** — templates sharpen per run, standards compound. Either that shows up in the data or template promotion is decoration.

### The measures
- **Rework rate** — does flow-structured work get redone less often than the same work done as plain tasks? The core "is a flow better than a list" question.
- **Rule-gap events per run, across runs of one template** — must TREND DOWN. This is the direct test of Q9's compounding claim (a rule-gap event = the human rejects at a gate where every check passed; see TDE-801).
- **Human review load stays FLAT as flow length grows** — the direct test of Q7's exception model. If review load scales with the number of steps rather than with problems found, exception-shaped review has failed and per-step approval has crept back in.

### The ANTI-measure
**Do NOT measure flow usage.** More flows created is not success. Q10 concluded most work should NOT be a flow, so a rising flow count is a warning sign that the scale floor is not holding — not a win. This matters because usage is the metric a product instinctively reaches for.
