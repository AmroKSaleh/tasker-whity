Requested by the owner 2026-08-08. Today both deletions REFUSE rather than cascade, which is safe but leaves no way to clear out a container you genuinely want gone.

CURRENT BEHAVIOUR
- deleteOrganization (lib/organizations.js, TDE-883) refuses while the org owns any environment, and names them.
- deleteEmptyEnvironment (lib/environments.js) refuses while the environment holds any project: "Move this environment's projects out before deleting it."
- deleteEnvironment (personal envs only) never destroys anything — it REASSIGNS projects to another environment, and refuses to delete the last one.

WHY A NAIVE CASCADE WOULD BE WRONG
The FKs already cascade in a way that quietly loses structure without losing rows: environments.org_id is ON DELETE CASCADE, and projects.environment_id is ON DELETE SET NULL. So deleting an org today would drop every project inside it into Unassigned with no warning — the projects survive, their placement does not.

THE DESIGN THAT FITS WHAT ALREADY EXISTS
Projects, tasks and flows all carry is_deleted + deleted_at and are restorable from the Recycle Bin for 7 days (pg_cron purge). Environments and organizations have NO such column.
So: SOFT-delete the projects (recoverable), HARD-delete the environments and the org. That makes "delete with contents" reversible for the part that holds real work, and irreversible only for cheap containers. Restored projects land in Unassigned, since their environment_id was nulled — a real, visible bucket rather than a dangling reference.

BLAST RADIUS JUSTIFIES A TYPED CONFIRMATION
An org can hold several environments holding several projects holding hundreds of tasks. A single window.confirm is not proportionate. The dialog must state the exact counts it is about to remove and require the container's name to be typed back.
