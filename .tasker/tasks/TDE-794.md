## THE ONE-SENTENCE ESSENCE — deferred from Q2, settled 2026-07-28 after the full agenda (user: "It is nice")

> **A flow is a single operation too big for one sitting — cut into steps so it holds together across its length, and gated only where one step's output becomes the next step's unexamined premise.**

What each clause carries:
- **"a single operation"** — Q10's container/operation line. It RUNS and TERMINATES. This is what stops a flow being a project, a section or a phase, all of which merely hold things indefinitely.
- **"too big for one sitting"** — Q1 (the root problem is SCALE/bigness) and Q2's scale floor. With gates optional, this clause carries the entire boundary against plain tasks.
- **"cut into steps"** — Q3's granularity rule: boundaries are located by what they must DO (hand an artifact over, change executor, permit a gate, permit a clean cold stop), not by size.
- **"so it holds together across its length"** — the HOLD IT TOGETHER motive (Q10): durable structure, resumability, legible progress, protection from drift and the context wall.
- **"and gated only where…"** — the RUN IT RIGHT motive (Q10), with **"only"** doing deliberate work: gates are optional, rare, and never the defining trait. This is the Q2 re-founding in one word.
- **"…one step's output becomes the next step's unexamined premise"** — Q4's seam, in its narrow form: AI-asserted context consumed WITHOUT re-derivation. Not every handoff — only the opaque ones.
